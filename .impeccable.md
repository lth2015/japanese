## Design Context

### Users

The primary user is the project author: a Chinese-speaking engineer with strong N2/N1 reading ability who wants faster, more natural spoken Japanese for work and daily life. The product is used during focused drills, as a passive second-screen display, and as printed reference material. For print artifacts, the job is quick scanning, repeated recall, and annotation by hand rather than linear textbook reading.

### Brand Personality

Calm, rigorous, and editorial. The interface should feel like a carefully typeset language reference made by a demanding teacher: approachable without becoming childish, modern without looking like a SaaS dashboard, and confident without visual noise.

### Aesthetic Direction

Print-first Swiss editorial minimalism with Japanese publishing cues. Use a warm paper-white ground, near-black text, one restrained teal accent, and vermilion only for corrections or high-risk pitfalls. Prefer Hiragino Mincho for large Japanese terms and Hiragino Sans for annotations. Avoid gradients, glass effects, decorative shadows, card grids, emoji, and low-contrast gray text. A3 landscape sheets must remain legible in grayscale and leave room for handwritten notes.

### Design Principles

1. Learning content is always the strongest visual layer; chrome and decoration recede.
2. Organize by retrieval: large Japanese form, nearby reading, concise Chinese meaning, then usage nuance.
3. Use lines, columns, and whitespace instead of nested cards; every separator must clarify structure.
4. Design at physical size: no essential text below 10.5 pt, strong contrast, and generous print-safe margins.
5. Preserve linguistic precision; do not trade correct Japanese, furigana, or caveats for visual symmetry.
